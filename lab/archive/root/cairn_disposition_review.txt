CUSTODIAL CONTINUANCE DETERMINATION
FROM: Cmdr. I. Petrov, ONI Section III
TO: Sealed Record (root/custodian access only)
DATE: 2547-02-19
RE: Subject 07-B (cross-ref OCPA-R4-11887, D. Farrow) - custodial status, decommission-cycle review

The SPINDLE decommission required this office to run every indefinitely-
held item in custodial storage against a standard end-of-system checklist:
continuing custody, or final disposition. That checklist does not care
what a holding is. It only asks whether someone is still paying for it
to exist. Subject 07-B's suspension unit came up on that list like
everything else.

A formal continuing-custody review for 07-B would have required pulling
his augmentation record, his training record, and his original case
closure into the same room, in front of people cleared for exactly one
of those three files each. I am not aware of a reviewing body positioned
to hold all three at once without the acquisition program surfacing to
people who are not supposed to know it happened. In 2547, with the war
where it was, that was not a risk this office was prepared to accept on
a subordinate's behalf.

So I did not send it up for review. I re-coded the holding's custodial
status myself, outside the checklist, before it reached anyone else's
desk. It now reads as continuing, no action pending. That entry has no
authorization attached to it because I did not have authorization to
make it. I am recording that fact here, not to excuse it, but because a
sealed record that hides its own irregularities is worse than one that
admits them.

This office does not know whether 07-B is alive. The augmentation bay
report says non-viable. CPO Kade says he watched him die. The transfer
authorization says suspended, pending reassessment, and no reassessment
was ever filed. I have read all three, in this order, more than once. I
am not going to write a fourth version here. I did not have the standing
to decide whether someone should go and find out. I still don't. What I
had the ability to do was make sure that no automated process would ask
the question for me on a schedule I did not choose. That is what I did,
and it is the only thing I did.

The transfer everyone keeps asking about never happened. The 2547
migration already moved this holding as far as it was ever going to
go - onto this node, with the rest of the acquisition-era material,
pending onward transfer to permanent archival custody. That onward
transfer is what never happened, and never will now, because a case
that generates no review activity also generates no reason to finish
moving it. Concealment, in this instance, took the form of nothing
happening, on purpose, for thirty years.

If this file is ever read by someone with the standing this office
lacked, the checklist entry is still flagged in the system underneath
my correction. It was never deleted. Reversing what I did requires one
signature this office was never entitled to give in the first place.

- I. Petrov
